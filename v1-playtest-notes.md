# Storm Chaser v1 — Playtest Notes

**Tester:** Mario, 3 yrs 9 mths (b. Oct 2022) · **Facilitator:** Ann
**Date:** 29 July 2026 · **Platform:** desktop browser, mouse
**Coverage:** all four sections completed

---

## Summary

The instrument collected data, but three problems ran through the whole session:

1. **Too much text at the wrong moment.** Design rationale was printed on screen during
   play. The facilitator needs to read, decide and record while managing a 3-year-old
   who is physically moving and losing interest. Explanation competes with execution.
2. **Not replayable.** Every quantity was fixed. On a second run the child recalls the
   answer rather than solving it, so the instrument cannot be used to measure change —
   which is most of the point of a diagnostic.
3. **Motor demands exceeded the child.** Several items required precise clicking. A
   3-year-old on a mouse cannot do this, so the item measured mouse control rather than
   the foundation.

---

## Section A — Load the Storm Truck

**A1. Tap-the-cars interaction failed.**
The child did not know where to tap. On a web page there is no affordance telling him
the cars are targets. The purpose of the tapping was also not explained to the
facilitator, who could not tell whether it mattered.
*Severity: high — the item did not measure Foundation 1.2.*

**A2. "Ask how many cars" follow-up was unclear.**
Too text-heavy to parse in the moment. The facilitator could not quickly work out what
she was being asked to observe or record.
*Severity: high — this is the most diagnostic item in the check.*

**A3. General text load.**
Long paragraphs on screen while supervising a restless child. Unusable under real
conditions.

## Section A — Flash the tornadoes

**A4. Rationale text unnecessary during play.**
The grey explanatory line adds nothing to executing the item. Belongs in the report,
where it aids interpretation.

**A5. Not replayable — fixed quantity.**
Tornado count was hard-coded. Second play, the child recalls "five" rather than
perceiving it. Subitizing specifically cannot be measured from memory.
*Severity: high — invalidates repeat testing.*

## Section A — Read the race numbers

**A6. Rationale text — same issue as A4.**

**A7. Tapping interaction unsuited to the platform.**

## Section B — Which Road?

**B1. Vocabulary gap on "fewer."**
Child has no meaning for the word. Needs either fixing or an explicit justification for
keeping it. *(Partially addressed mid-session; see revision report.)*

**B2. Instructions too long.** Keep only what is needed to run the item.

**B3. Not replayable — fixed car counts.** Child memorises which road.

## Section B — The tornado blows one away

**B4. The greyed-out car is not a readable cue.**
A 3-year-old does not interpret reduced opacity as "gone." He sees a faded car, which is
still a car. The subtraction was therefore never presented.
*Severity: high — the item did not measure Foundation 2.1.*

## Section B — Garage parking

**B5. No visual anchor for the total.**
The child is asked to reason about five cars while only three are visible.

**B6. Mixing numerals with pictured quantities is confusing.**
Answer options were written numerals while the problem was pictures of cars. This turns
a quantity question into a numeral-recognition question — which is already measured
separately in Foundation 1.5.

No specific problems raised. *(Text load and randomisation issues apply generally.)*

## Section D — Name the road signs

**D1. Tapping interaction unsuited to the platform.**

**D2. Is naming shapes a mathematics problem or a vocabulary problem?**
Raised as a design challenge to the instrument's validity.

**D3. Why does knowing a car is "under the bridge" matter?**
Same challenge, applied to position language.

## Section D — Turn the car and park it

**D4. The item was not comprehensible to the facilitator.**
Purpose unclear from the screen.

**D5. The child could not act on it.**
Required repeated button presses plus a commit action, with a mouse. Nothing he could
meaningfully do.

**D6. Underlying construct unjustified on screen.**
No indication why rotation belongs in a maths check.
*Severity: high — item produced no usable data.*

---

## Report

**R1. Does not update on replay without a page refresh.**
Re-running a section did not replace the earlier result, so repeat play produced stale
or merged output.
*Severity: high — a diagnostic that cannot be re-run is a one-shot instrument.*

**R2. Notes are not interpretable.**
For every criterion the report should state: what the item measures, why it is designed
that way, what learning objective it assesses, and — in the notes — what this particular
child's result actually indicates.

---

## Facilitator's overall verdict

The item bank is sound; the delivery is not. The instrument was written for a reader
with time to think, and administered by a parent with neither hand free.
